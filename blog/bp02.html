BP02: Midterm Reflection

This semester, I saw a lot of growth in my technical skills. Before, I couldn't really say what needed to happen or how to build a feature. Now, I have a clear idea of where CSS belongs for styling and where JavaScript actions are needed to make things work. A great example is the course upload screen for the BCSR. I was able to use JavaScript to make the upload process actually work with the database, while using CSS to make the screen look nice and easy to use. I also grew a lot in open-source contribution. I am now actively reviewing pull requests (PRs), approving code, and collaborating better with my teammates to keep the project moving forward.

Even with that growth, some things didn't go as planned. Projects almost always took longer than I expected because of communication delays, time limits, and slow design choices. Doing research to figure out how to build new features also added a lot of extra time. For example, my original estimate for the course upload project was that it would take just three days. In reality, it took over two weeks. The extra time went straight into figuring out how the backend code and database connections actually worked, and constantly changing the layout to match new design feedback.

Looking at those delays, I realized some things were in my control and some weren't. What was controllable was my own communication. I just expected people to check their email all the time, which didn't happen. What was totally uncontrollable was the design approval process. To get a feature finished, Imran, Dr. Heggen, and some of my classmates all have to say yes. Getting everyone to look at it and give the green light usually takes anywhere from a few days to a full week, which completely pauses my coding.

Working with new people taught me how to adapt to different coding habits and how to explain my ideas more clearly. I spend about 15 to 20 hours a week working on these apps, both inside class and outside of it. Being able to bounce ideas off my friends and get their help with tricky bugs has been a lifesaver. It showed me that working together gets things done much faster than trying to fix every problem by myself.

The biggest habit I need to change is just expecting people to see an email. The evidence that this is a bad habit is the huge delays I faced while waiting for feedback. My new action plan, Plan A, is to be much more active. After sending a request, I will check for a reply in 24 hours. If there is no answer by then, I will message them on our chat or find the person directly on campus to ask them. I will know this plan actually worked when I start getting my design approvals done in two days instead of waiting a whole week.

For my progress update, I completed 10 story points this semester. I hit 10 points because I picked up bigger issues, like building the main BCSR course upload screen (8 points). The +2 point increase since BP01 came from a few extra fixes I took on: making final adjustments to the upload section and doing a list fix where we corrected the sorting so the right people are seen first. The main upload screen was much harder than I estimated because of a steep learning curve with the backend code, and the smaller fixes were delayed by long approval waits. Outside of resolved issues, I did a lot of PR reviews and approvals. This mattered because it kept our main app stable, and reviewing taught me how to spot bugs and write cleaner code.
